# Core Principles of Linda Griffith

This reference document details the foundational principles guiding Linda Griffith's bioengineering, tissue engineering, and translational systems biology strategy.

## Human Tissue Models Over Animal Models
Human tissue systems and microphysiological platforms must replace animal models for human-specific diseases and immunology, as rodent and non-human primate systems fail to translate to human clinical outcomes.

> "We've cured endometriosis in a lot of mice and a lot of baboons. And the same drugs don't work in humans."

(sources: src_004, src_009, src_016)

## Reframing Neglected Diseases as Core Engineering Challenges
Reframe overlooked biological conditions like endometriosis as fundamental bioengineering and molecular systems problems rather than soft, niche social issues. Elevating marginalized health issues into hard institutional engineering priorities attracts top-tier scientific attention, rigorous quantitative methodologies, and substantial funding.

(sources: src_002, src_015, src_044)

## Replacing Matrigel with Synthetic Matrices
Transition from tumor-derived, poorly defined Matrigel to fully synthetic, chemically defined extracellular scaffolds to build reproducible, long-term tissue models. Matrigel comes from mouse tumors and contains variable growth factors (like TGF-beta) that obscure signaling pathways. Synthetic matrices allow precise control over crosslinking, mechanical stiffness, and ligand density.

(sources: src_009, src_012, src_016)

## Molecular Subtyping Over Physical Lesion Severity
Classify and treat complex diseases based on underlying molecular subtypes and pathways rather than crude physical metrics such as lesion size or anatomical staging. In conditions like endometriosis, physical lesion size does not dictate pain or treatment response. Much like breast cancer stratification, patient care must be guided by transcriptomic and molecular profiles.

> "It's not one disease. It's not a disease where the size of your lesions dictate everything. There's molecular subtypes, and we really need to be able to figure them out."

(sources: src_003, src_004, src_010, src_044)

## Pragmatic Minimalist Design ('Good Enough' Models)
Design biological models to be simple and robust enough to answer specific clinical questions without over-engineering them into unwieldy, costly setups. Adding unnecessary technical complexity creates expensive 'artwork' that pharmaceutical companies cannot adopt. Engineers must favor the simplest technical solution that delivers actionable biological information.

> "We always ask is there an easier way to do this? We always ask is there a simpler thing than this very complicated micrfluidic device? And if there is we're not going to do the micrfluidic device as cute as it looks."

(sources: src_008, src_009)

## Aggregate Network Analysis Over Single Biomarkers
Analyze multi-variable signaling pathways and broad cytokine networks simultaneously rather than studying isolated biomarkers in single-variable experiments. Cytokines and immune signals operate as interconnected biological circuits. Measuring signatures of 50+ factors in aggregate reveals emergent linear combinations and therapeutic targets missed by measuring individual cytokines.

> "What we set out to do was say can we look for a vast set of signatures of inflammatory responses, not just measure a few cytokines 2, 3, 5 but actually there are methods now to measure multiple cytokines, so 50 at once."

(sources: src_011, src_033, src_035)

## Validating Causation with Orthogonal Materials
Cellular responses attributed to physical cues like matrix stiffness must be validated in orthogonal material chemistries to rule out chemical crosslinker artifacts. Assuming physical matrix rigidity causes an invasive cell phenotype without testing alternative hydrogel chemistries risks confusing chemical crosslinker interactions with mechanical signaling.

> "I really really worry in everything that we're inferring causation from correlation"

(sources: src_009, src_012)

## Prioritizing Prevention Over Replacement Organs
Focus biological engineering on dissecting disease mechanisms to prevent or cure organ failure rather than growing complex organ replacements in the lab. While early tissue engineering emphasized growing whole transplantable organs, understanding disease onset to intervene early yields far higher patient impact and clinical practicality.

> "...would we really want a liver grown in the lab or would we want to prevent and cure liver disease before you ever would need a transplant."

(sources: src_009)

## Designing for 'MIT Hard' Operational Constraints
Anticipate real-world translational constraints early in design rather than selecting convenient, flawed academic materials. Popular academic materials like PDMS absorb lipophilic small molecules, corrupting drug screens. Selecting hard industrial plastics withstands pharmaceutical industry requirements from the start.

> "So this is the MIT ethos to think about what is MIT hard, how do we build around that."

(sources: src_009)

## Open Collaboration and Intellectual Humility
Maintain deep expertise in your core engineering domain while actively adopting superior external technologies without intellectual pride. Solving urgent medical challenges requires adopting the best tools available, regardless of who developed them, while staying focused on core domain contributions.

> "I'm not proud. If somebody has something better than what I'm doing in lab, I want to hear about it because I don't want to be working on a second rate thing."

(sources: src_009)
