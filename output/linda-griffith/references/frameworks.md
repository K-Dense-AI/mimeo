# Frameworks of Linda Griffith

This document describes key operational frameworks for tissue engineering, microphysiological platform design, and quantitative pharmacological modeling.

## Microphysiological System (MPS) Patient Benchmarking Framework
A systematic workflow for building, running, and computationally validating human organ-on-a-chip models against in vivo patient phenotypes.

### Steps
1. Co-culture patient organoids and stromal cells inside synthetic matrices.
2. Integrate microvascular channels and perfuse human immune cells (monocytes/macrophages).
3. Control and compute endocrine/metabolic parameters (e.g., steroid hormone dynamics).
4. Benchmark multi-omic and transcriptomic output signatures against stratified patient clinical cohorts.

> "how do we decide that our in vitro models are capturing in vivo phenotypes? We're accruing lots of transcriptomic data and our mathematical modeling collaborators... has ways to look at these signatures..."

(sources: src_009, src_016)

## Patient Avatar Disease Translation Cycle
An iterative pipeline linking deep clinical phenotyping with bioengineered human tissue avatars to discover targeted therapeutics.

### Steps
1. Perform deep physiological and molecular phenotyping on patient cohorts.
2. Construct 3D human tissue models ('patient avatars') using patient biopsies.
3. Run biological experiments through avatars to uncover root mechanisms.
4. Stratify molecular disease subtypes and test targeted therapeutics matched to patient subgroups.

> "Our vision is that we combine really great clinical phenotyping at the physiological level and at the molecular level with this kind of modeling I described and then cycle through these living patient avatars until we can come up with um mechanistic understanding of the disease..."

(sources: src_009)

## Quantitative Systems Pharmacology (QSP)
A framework incorporating spatial and temporal physics into molecular dynamics to model complex drug-cell interactions.

### Steps
1. Map receptor binding and therapeutic molecular targets.
2. Model cell dynamics over space and time (signaling, endosomal internalization, recycling, degradation).
3. Combine deterministic mechanistic models with spatial physics to optimize drug behavior (e.g., pH-dependent detachment inside endosomes).

(sources: src_009)
